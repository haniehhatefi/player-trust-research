# Player Trust Research
**Fairness Perception and Trust Erosion in Competitive Mobile Gaming**

A longitudinal qualitative study and a related cross-cultural analysis of player discourse in competitive mobile backgammon environments (2022–2026).

- [Longitudinal preprint](https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/gzsr7_v1)
- [Cross-cultural preprint](https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/rjnfz_v1)
- [Portfolio case study](https://haniehhatefi.it/player-trust-research/)

> **Confidentiality:** The client organisation and specific game are intentionally anonymised. The primary product is described as a Persian-language competitive mobile backgammon game. No client or product identity is disclosed.

## Overview
This repository documents two related but distinct manuscripts:
1. A five-year longitudinal qualitative study of Persian-language player reviews and anonymised customer-support interactions (2022–2026).
2. A cross-cultural analysis comparing the Persian-language context with a stratified sample of negative anglophone app-store reviews.

Both manuscripts use Inductive Thematic Analysis (ITA). They investigate player-generated accounts and perceptions; they do **not** technically test the game's random-number generator, establish manipulation, or prove player allegations.

## Corpus and sampling overview

| Material | Reported scope |
|---|---|
| Persian-language review corpus | More than 52,000 publicly accessible reviews |
| Persian-language detailed qualitative sample | Longitudinal manuscript: approximately 500 review and support items; cross-cultural manuscript: approximately 500 reviews selected for detailed coding |
| Supplementary support material | Anonymised customer-support interactions under a research collaboration agreement |
| Anglophone source corpus | 4,116 one- and two-star reviews |
| Anglophone analytic sample | Stratified random sample of 750 reviews, 150 per year (2022–2026) |

The sampling frames and denominators differ. The longitudinal manuscript describes the same calendar month and 30-day period in each year for the Persian-language data. The cross-cultural manuscript specifies October for the Persian-language corpus and a stratified sample drawn across the year for the anglophone corpus. Percentages from the two contexts must not be treated as directly comparable.

## Research questions
The manuscripts ask related but not identical questions. The longitudinal study examines how fairness-related themes, complaint framing, and complexity evolve over five years, and how platform interactions may reinforce or mitigate distrust. The cross-cultural study additionally compares themes, discourse patterns, and institutional-response dynamics across Persian-language and anglophone contexts. See the individual study documents for source-specific questions.

## Seven operational themes
1. Dice Manipulation
2. Pay-to-Win Mechanics
3. Bot Opponents
4. Connectivity Sabotage
5. Predetermined Outcomes
6. Statistical Anomaly Reporting
7. Support Dismissal / Perceived Support Dismissal

The cross-cultural manuscript groups the themes into Perceived Agency Erosion, Trust Erosion, and Statistical Misinterpretation. The longitudinal manuscript uses Perceived Agency, Trust Erosion, and Statistical Misinterpretation. Their theme-to-meta-theme mappings are not fully consistent; this repository records each separately rather than harmonising them. See [Analytical Framework](docs/analytical-framework.md).

## Annual-rate discrepancy between the manuscripts
Both manuscripts report 3% for 2022 and 18% for 2026, but differ on intermediate Persian-language annual values:

| Year | Longitudinal manuscript | Cross-cultural manuscript |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2022 | 3% | 3% |
| 2023 | 8% | 5% |
| 2024 | 10% | 8% |
| 2025 | 17% | 12% |
| 2026 | 18% | 18% |

The source materials available for this repository do not include the underlying calculation sheet needed to resolve this difference. Source-specific values are retained in each study document. Only the shared endpoints are used in the summary figure; no corrected intermediate series is asserted.

## What the research does and does not establish
**It documents:** player discourse, reported perceptions, recurring explanations of unfairness, and accounts of platform communication.

**It does not establish:** technical manipulation, the accuracy of individual player allegations, causal effects of support responses, or population-wide prevalence of distrust. The studies are qualitative and observational.
