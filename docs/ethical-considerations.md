# Ethical Considerations and Anonymisation

## Client and product confidentiality
The client organisation and specific game must remain anonymous in all public repository materials. The client has authorised an anonymised public presentation. This permission is limited to that presentation and does not transfer ownership or grant general reuse rights.

Use only generic descriptions:
- “the gaming client” or “the client” for the organisation;
- “a Persian-language competitive mobile backgammon game” for the primary product;
- “an anglophone competitive mobile backgammon application” for the comparative product.

Do not add identifying company or game names, store links, product screenshots, proprietary assets, internal documents, or details that could reasonably reveal the client or product.

## User privacy
The manuscripts report that usernames were removed and that support interactions were provided in anonymised form. This repository does not publish raw reviews, support transcripts, account information, usernames, contact details, or other personal data.

Exact review quotations are omitted because public text may remain searchable and linkable to an individual.

## Interpretation of allegations
The research reports player perceptions and allegations concerning dice, opponents, connectivity, monetisation, and predetermined outcomes. These are not independently verified technical findings. Use wording such as “players perceived”, “players alleged”, or “reviews described”; do not state that the game manipulated outcomes.

## Limits of permission
Permission to publish the project anonymously does not necessarily authorise redistribution of raw or compiled datasets, private support conversations, client-owned logos or screenshots, internal documents, or confidential commercial information.

## Public repository checks
Before publication, review Markdown, tables, alt text, captions, Mermaid labels, filenames, commit messages, branch names, release notes, image metadata, links, and Git history. A clean ZIP does not establish that previous commits in a Git repository are free of confidential material.

## Research integrity
The two manuscripts contain differences in intermediate annual complaint rates and one meta-theme mapping. These are disclosed rather than silently harmonised. The repository distinguishes source-reported findings from interpretation and design implications. Both preprints state that they have not undergone peer review.
