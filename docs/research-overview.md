# Research Overview

## Scope
The project comprises two related manuscripts examining how players describe fairness, perceived manipulation, and trust in competitive mobile backgammon environments.

- **Study 1:** a five-year longitudinal qualitative analysis of Persian-language player discourse and anonymised support interactions (2022–2026).
- **Study 2:** a cross-cultural comparative analysis using the Persian-language context and an anglophone review sample.

The research analyses perceptions and discourse. It does not test the technical fairness of the game systems.

## Rationale
Competitive games combining skill and chance can produce outcomes that players find difficult to interpret. When outcomes appear inconsistent with expectations, players may describe them as unfair, manipulated, predetermined, or influenced by commercial or platform decisions. The manuscripts examine how such accounts are expressed and how recurring themes relate to agency, trust, probability, and institutional communication.

## Research questions
The manuscripts have related but distinct questions.

### Longitudinal manuscript
1. What fairness-related themes emerge from player discourse, and how can they be understood in relation to psychological and cognitive constructs?
2. How do the frequency, framing, and complexity of fairness-related complaints evolve over five years?
3. What cognitive and emotional processes contribute to player distrust, and at what points might platform interactions mitigate or reinforce that trajectory?

### Cross-cultural manuscript
1. What fairness-related themes emerge from negative reviews of competitive mobile backgammon games?
2. How have fairness-related complaints evolved in the Persian-language corpus between 2022 and 2026?
3. How do players describe perceived manipulation and unfairness, and what role do platform interactions appear to play?
4. To what extent do themes and institutional-response dynamics also appear in an anglophone player community, and where do differences emerge?

## Research materials
The manuscripts report more than 52,000 Persian-language reviews. The longitudinal manuscript reports approximately 500 review and support data items retained for detailed coding; the cross-cultural manuscript reports approximately 500 Persian-language reviews selected for detailed coding, alongside supplementary support transcripts. The cross-cultural manuscript also reports 4,116 anglophone one- and two-star reviews and a stratified sample of 750 anglophone reviews. Study-specific sampling details are provided in [Methodology](methodology.md).

## Analytical outputs
Seven operational themes are reported: Dice Manipulation; Pay-to-Win Mechanics; Bot Opponents; Connectivity Sabotage; Predetermined Outcomes; Statistical Anomaly Reporting; and Support Dismissal (called Perceived Support Dismissal in the longitudinal manuscript).

Both manuscripts describe three meta-themes, but their theme memberships differ in one mapping. The longitudinal manuscript also presents a ten-stage Player Journey of Distrust. See [Analytical Framework](analytical-framework.md).

## Interpretation boundaries
The research documents player claims and interpretations. It does not establish that alleged manipulation occurred, nor does it independently validate RNG, matchmaking, opponent identity, connectivity, or monetisation systems. Findings about communication and trust are observational, not causal.
