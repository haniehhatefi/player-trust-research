# Study 2 — Perceived Manipulation, Fairness, and Trust: A Cross-Cultural Analysis

**Source:** [OSF preprint, DOI 10.31235/osf.io/rjnfz_v1](https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/rjnfz_v1)  
**Manuscript date:** October 2026 · **Review status:** Not peer reviewed, as stated in the manuscript.

## Purpose
This study examines fairness-related discourse across Persian-language and anglophone competitive mobile backgammon contexts. It compares player accounts of perceived manipulation, fairness, and trust, as well as complaint framing and platform-response structures. It does not independently verify allegations about game mechanics.

## Corpus A: Persian-language context
The manuscript reports more than 52,000 reviews posted between October 2022 and October 2026 from two Iranian Android app distribution platforms. Sampling focused on one-star and two-star reviews posted within a consistent 30-day October window in each year. Reviews required at least 15 words of substantive gameplay commentary. Installation issues, support requests, feature requests, and off-topic content were excluded. Approximately 500 reviews were selected for detailed coding across five annual cohorts. An anonymised customer-support transcript corpus was also included under a research collaboration agreement.

## Corpus B: Anglophone context
The source corpus comprised 4,116 one-star and two-star reviews from an anglophone app-store listing in an Australian market. A stratified random sample of 750 reviews was selected, 150 per year from 2022 to 2026. The manuscript reports a fixed random seed of 42 and a minimum of 15 words of gameplay-relevant content. The 2026 cohort includes reviews through 6 October 2026. The app and organisation are not identified in this repository.

## Analysis
The seven-theme framework was developed from the Persian-language corpus and then used in the anglophone analysis. For the anglophone corpus, the manuscript reports AI-assisted first-pass keyword classification to generate provisional theme flags. The classifications were reviewed and interpreted qualitatively; they were preliminary aids, not final coding decisions. The manuscript cautions that automated detection may identify explicit language more readily than latent meanings or contextual nuances. Frequency estimates should therefore be interpreted cautiously.

The Persian-language analysis was conducted in the original language. The manuscript reports bilingual back-translation to check semantic consistency of translated quotations.

## Seven operational themes
T01 Dice Manipulation; T02 Pay-to-Win Mechanics; T03 Bot Opponents; T04 Connectivity Sabotage; T05 Predetermined Outcomes; T06 Statistical Anomaly Reporting; T07 Support Dismissal.

The manuscript reports dice-manipulation beliefs as the dominant theme in both contexts and discusses differences in complaint trajectories, cultural framing, and institutional response structures.

## Developer-response patterns
The manuscript reports an approximately 99% reply rate for the 750 sampled anglophone reviews. It categorises responses as generic empathy/fairness assurance (≈26%), explicit RNG explanation/fairness claim (≈10%), redirection to external support (≈9%), hybrid combinations (≈24%), and personalised or substantially customised responses (≈31%). It states that approximately 69% fell within the four template categories. These figures are specific to the sample and coding described in the manuscript.

## Cross-cultural interpretation and limitations
The manuscript reports convergence in the prominence of dice-manipulation narratives and the use of observational or statistical reasoning. It also reports differences in complaint trajectories, framing, and communication structures.

The manuscript explicitly cautions that Persian-language and anglophone quantitative measures are not directly comparable because their sampling and reporting procedures differ. The comparison is primarily thematic and interpretive.

Limitations include different sampling frames, a single anglophone market context, AI-assisted preliminary coding, the focus on negative reviews, observational design, and researcher positionality.
