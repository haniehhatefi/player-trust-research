# Study 2 — Perceived Manipulation, Fairness, and Trust in Competitive Mobile Gaming: A Cross-Cultural Analysis of Player Discourse

**Source:** [OSF preprint, DOI 10.31235/osf.io/rjnfz_v1](https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/rjnfz_v1)  
**Manuscript date:** October 2026  
**Review status:** Not peer reviewed, as stated in the manuscript.

## Purpose

This study examines fairness-related discourse across Persian-language and anglophone competitive mobile backgammon contexts. It compares player accounts of perceived manipulation, fairness, and trust, as well as complaint framing and platform-response structures. It does not independently verify allegations about game mechanics.

## Corpus A: Persian-language context

The manuscript reports more than 52,000 reviews posted between October 2022 and October 2026 from two Iranian Android app distribution platforms.

Sampling focused on one-star and two-star reviews posted within a consistent 30-day October window in each year. Reviews required at least 15 words of substantive gameplay commentary. Installation issues, support requests, feature requests, and off-topic content were excluded.

Approximately 500 reviews were selected for detailed coding across five annual cohorts. An anonymised customer-support transcript corpus was also included under a research collaboration agreement. These support materials are restricted and are not distributed in this repository.

The annual Persian-language complaint rates reported in this manuscript differ from those in the separate longitudinal manuscript. These source-specific figures are reproduced without reconciliation; see [Data Availability](../DATA-AVAILABILITY.md) for the documented discrepancy.

## Corpus B: Anglophone context

The source corpus comprised 4,116 one-star and two-star reviews from an anglophone app-store listing in an Australian market.

A stratified random sample of 750 reviews was selected, with 150 reviews per year from 2022 to 2026. The manuscript reports a fixed random seed of 42 and a minimum of 15 words of gameplay-relevant content.

The 2026 cohort includes reviews posted through 6 October 2026, the date of data collection. The application and organisation are not identified in this repository.

## Analysis

The seven-theme framework was developed from the Persian-language corpus and then used in the anglophone analysis.

For the anglophone corpus, the manuscript reports AI-assisted first-pass keyword classification to generate provisional theme flags. The classifications were reviewed and interpreted qualitatively; they were preliminary aids, not final coding decisions.

The manuscript cautions that automated detection may identify explicit language more readily than latent meanings or contextual nuances. Frequency estimates should therefore be interpreted cautiously.

The Persian-language analysis was conducted in the original language. The manuscript reports bilingual back-translation to check semantic consistency of translated quotations.

## Seven operational themes

The manuscript identifies seven recurring operational themes:

| Code | Theme | Description |
|---|---|---|
| T01 | Dice Manipulation | Belief that random outcomes are not genuinely random. |
| T02 | Pay-to-Win Mechanics | Belief that expenditure influences competitive outcomes. |
| T03 | Bot Opponents | Suspicion that opponents presented as human are automated. |
| T04 | Connectivity Sabotage | Interpretation of technical disruptions as disadvantaging players. |
| T05 | Predetermined Outcomes | Belief that outcomes are decided independently of player actions. |
| T06 | Statistical Anomaly Reporting | Use of unusual outcome sequences as evidence of manipulation. |
| T07 | Support Dismissal | Perception that platform responses dismiss player concerns. |

The manuscript reports dice-manipulation beliefs as the dominant theme in both contexts and discusses differences in complaint trajectories, cultural framing, and institutional response structures.

| T03 Bot Opponents | ≈9% |

The manuscript also reports year-level percentages for T01, T02, T03, and T06. Consult Table 3 in the preprint for the complete source table.

A dash in the source table indicates that no year-level percentage was reported for that theme; it should not be interpreted as a zero frequency.

These figures are AI-assisted and indicative, as described in the manuscript. They should not be treated as definitive human-coded prevalence estimates.

## Developer-response patterns

The manuscript reports an approximately 99% reply rate for the 750 sampled anglophone reviews.

It categorises developer responses into the following patterns:

| Response category | Approximate prevalence |
|---|---:|
| Generic empathy and fairness assurance | 26% |
| Explicit RNG explanation and fairness claim | 10% |
| Redirection to external support channels | 9% |
| Hybrid combinations of the above patterns | 24% |
| Personalised or substantially customised responses | 31% |

The four template categories together account for approximately 69% of responses. The remaining approximately 31% were classified as personalised or substantially customised.

These figures are specific to the sample and coding described in the manuscript. They should not be interpreted as representative of all developer responses across mobile games or app-store platforms.

## Meta-themes reported in this manuscript

The manuscript organises the seven operational themes into three higher-order meta-themes:

| Meta-theme | Theme membership |
|---|---|
| Perceived Agency Erosion | T01, T04, T05 |
| Trust Erosion | T02, T03, T07 |
| Statistical Misinterpretation | T01, T05, T06 |

This mapping differs from the longitudinal manuscript in the membership of the Perceived Agency meta-theme. Both versions are recorded separately in this repository rather than harmonised.

## Player Journey of Distrust

The manuscript proposes a ten-stage interpretive model describing recurring patterns through which perceptions of unfairness may develop and persist:

1. First notable losing streak
2. Asymmetric dice observation
3. First suspicion of manipulation
4. Active pattern-seeking begins
5. Perceived confirmation obtained
6. Support contact initiated
7. Generic support response received
8. Public review written
9. Temporary withdrawal or suspension of play
10. Return to play

The model is cyclical rather than strictly linear. It does not describe a universal sequence followed by every player. The manuscript identifies stages 03, 05, 07, and 09 as potentially important points for design, communication, or support interventions.

Stage 07 is discussed as a point at which generic or repetitive support responses may be interpreted as further evidence of dismissal or concealment. This describes reported player interpretations, not proof of the platform's intentions.

## Cross-cultural interpretation

The manuscript reports convergence in the prominence of dice-manipulation narratives and the use of observational or statistical reasoning across both contexts.

It also reports differences in complaint trajectories, framing, and communication structures. Persian-language reviews frequently draw upon themes such as fate, destiny, injustice, and honour, while anglophone reviews more commonly use the language of fraud, consumer rights, and commercial legitimacy.

The manuscript interprets these differences as culturally and contextually situated forms of expressing concerns about fairness. They should not be treated as fixed characteristics of all Persian-speaking or anglophone players.

## Comparison and quantitative limitations

The manuscript explicitly cautions that Persian-language and anglophone quantitative measures are not directly comparable.

The Persian-language corpus uses a purposive October sampling window and reports fairness-related complaint prevalence relative to one-star reviews. The anglophone corpus uses a stratified sample of negative reviews drawn across the calendar year and reports theme frequencies within that sample.

The two sampling frames and denominators differ. The comparison is therefore primarily thematic and interpretive, rather than a direct comparison of numerical prevalence.

The Persian-language annual rates reported in this manuscript are:

| Year | Fairness-related complaint rate |
|---|---:|
| 2022 | 3% |
| 2023 | 5% |
| 2024 | 8% |
| 2025 | 12% |
| 2026 | 18% |

These values are reproduced as reported in this manuscript. The separate longitudinal manuscript reports different intermediate values. The discrepancy remains unresolved in the available materials; see [Data Availability](../DATA-AVAILABILITY.md).

## Interpretation and limitations

The manuscript argues that fairness complaints can be understood as expressions of broader concerns about trust, platform legitimacy, and communication, rather than solely as reports of technical malfunction.

It discusses transparency, contextual information about probabilities, clearer signals of opponent identity, personalised support communication, and monitoring of emerging fairness narratives as design considerations.

The findings do not establish that game outcomes were manipulated, that individual player allegations were technically accurate, or that particular communication practices caused trust erosion.

Limitations reported in the manuscript include:

- Different sampling frames and procedures across the Persian-language and anglophone corpora.
- A single anglophone application listing within one national market (Australia).
- AI-assisted preliminary coding of the anglophone corpus and the limitations of keyword-based theme detection.
- The focus on negative reviews, which excludes satisfied, neutral, and non-reviewing players.
- The observational qualitative design, which does not permit causal inference.
- Researcher positionality and the possibility of interpretive bias.
- The focus on competitive mobile backgammon, which limits assumptions about generalisability to other game genres and communities.

Both the sampling limitations and the preliminary nature of AI-assisted classification should be considered when interpreting the reported frequencies and cross-cultural findings.
