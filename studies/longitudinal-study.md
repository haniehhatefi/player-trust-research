# Study 1 — Fairness Perception and Trust Erosion in Competitive Mobile Gaming: A Five-Year Longitudinal Study

**Source:** [OSF preprint, DOI 10.31235/osf.io/gzsr7_v1](https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/gzsr7_v1)  
**Manuscript date:** October 2026 · **Review status:** Not peer reviewed, as stated in the manuscript.

## Purpose
This study examines how players construct, reinforce, and communicate beliefs about unfairness in a competitive mobile gaming environment over 2022–2026. It focuses on player discourse, reviews, and supplementary anonymised customer-support interactions. It does not technically test or establish manipulation of game outcomes.

## Data and sampling
The manuscript reports more than 52,000 reviews collected across multiple mobile app marketplaces. Reviews were gathered from the same calendar month and 30-day period in each study year.

The purposive sample included one-star and two-star reviews with at least 15 words of substantive content relevant to gameplay, fairness, outcomes, trust, or perceived system behaviour. Reviews focused exclusively on installation issues, feature requests, account problems, or unrelated service concerns were excluded. Approximately 500 review and support data items were retained for detailed coding across five annual cohorts. Supplementary anonymised support interactions were provided under a research collaboration agreement.

## Analysis
The manuscript reports the six phases of thematic analysis: familiarisation, initial coding, theme development, theme review, theme definition/refinement, and production of the analysis. Each annual cohort was analysed independently before cross-cohort comparison. Coding was conducted in the source language; English translation followed theme development. Reflexive notes and analytical audit trails are reported.

## Annual fairness-related complaint rate
Table 1 of the longitudinal manuscript reports the following rates as a proportion of one-star reviews:

| Year | Rate |
|---|---:|
| 2022 | 3% |
| 2023 | 8% |
| 2024 | 10% |
| 2025 | 17% |
| 2026 | 18% |

These values are reproduced as reported in this manuscript. The cross-cultural manuscript reports different intermediate values for the Persian-language series. The discrepancy is unresolved in the available materials; see [Data Availability](../DATA-AVAILABILITY.md). The longitudinal manuscript interprets the increase as greater prominence of fairness-related complaints in the review corpus, not as proof of a particular cause.

## Seven operational themes

| Code | Theme | Description reported in the manuscript |
|---|---|---|
| T01 | Dice Manipulation | Belief that random outcomes are not genuinely random. |
| T02 | Pay-to-Win Mechanics | Belief that expenditure influences competitive outcomes. |
| T03 | Bot Opponents | Suspicion that opponents presented as human are automated. |
| T04 | Connectivity Sabotage | Interpretation of technical disruptions as disadvantaging players. |
| T05 | Predetermined Outcomes | Belief that outcomes are decided independently of player actions. |
| T06 | Statistical Anomaly Reporting | Use of unusual outcome sequences as evidence of manipulation. |
| T07 | Perceived Support Dismissal | Perception that platform responses dismiss player concerns. |

The manuscript describes T01 as present throughout and the most frequently observed theme. It describes trajectories for other themes but does not provide a complete numerical frequency table for all seven.

## Meta-themes reported in this manuscript

| Meta-theme | Theme membership |

| Perceived Agency | T01, T05, T06 |

The same membership is printed for Perceived Agency and Statistical Misinterpretation. This is reproduced faithfully and flagged for author clarification; no correction is inferred.

## Ten-stage Player Journey of Distrust
The manuscript proposes this interpretive model:
1. First notable losing streak
2. Asymmetric outcome observation
3. First suspicion of manipulation
4. Active pattern-seeking begins
5. Perceived confirmation obtained
6. Support contact initiated
7. Perceived support dismissal
8. Public complaint or warning
9. Temporary disengagement
10. Return to play

Stages 03, 05, 07, and 09 are identified in the manuscript as particularly important transition points. The model is not a deterministic sequence experienced by every player.

## Interpretation and limitations
The manuscript argues that fairness complaints may be understood as expressions of declining trust, not only as reports of technical malfunction. It discusses transparency, probability communication, player communication, and support systems as design considerations.

Limitations include the focus on negative reviews, a single game context, the fixed annual sampling window, and the observational qualitative design. The study does not establish causality or generalisability to all games and players.
